## A-01 "Homogeneous equations" (IA-DE-012)

- **Where:** Schedules 2026-27, Differential Equations, within the paragraph on higher-order linear equations (printed p.9). The Schedules give the two words and nothing more.
- **Why it is ambiguous:** "homogeneous" is used in two standard ways in differential equations: (i) a linear equation with no forcing term; (ii) a first-order equation of the type that the substitution of a
  ratio of the variables reduces to a separable one (often called "equidimensional"). The Schedules do not say which is meant, and the position inside the higher-order paragraph is an inference at most, not a
  statement by Cambridge.
- **Status: UNRESOLVED.** The director's decision (D-20) is not to equate it silently with the equidimensional reading and to leave it open until Cambridge teaching material (lecture notes or an example sheet from the
  course) clarifies it.
- **How the repository handles it:** the item summary says only that the equations are named and the class is unspecified. IA-DE-012 has two `connection` links (to IA-DE-007 and IA-DE-011) so that neither reading
  is privileged, and no reading resource is assigned to it ([reading-alignment.md](reading-alignment.md) marks it unknown).
- **What would resolve it:** the course's own lecture notes or the Faculty example sheets for Differential Equations (verification task B7 and B10 in [verification-tasks.md](../references/verification-tasks.md)).
