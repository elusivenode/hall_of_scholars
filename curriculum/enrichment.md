# Harvard Math 55 enrichment map

**Everything here is optional enrichment. None of it is Cambridge-required.** The Cambridge Schedules
are the authority for what Part IA covers ([syllabus.md](syllabus.md)). Enrichment is taken after the
relevant Cambridge material, never instead of it and never at the expense of the example sheets.

Retrieved 21/10/2026. Two Harvard resources were supplied and they are **different courses**. They are
documented separately and must not be conflated.

Access limitations: Math 55 is "only open to first-year Harvard College students". The syllabus pages
and topic lists are public; lecture notes and problem sets are linked from the Canvas pages and were
**not** opened (they may require a Harvard login). The page says electronic copies of the texts, other
than Artin and Dummit and Foote, are free "to Harvard students" via Springer, so that is not a public
route. Course AI policy: the 55a page says it "prohibits the use of AI tools to find or write up
solutions to homework problems". The same principle applies in this repository: an AI-supplied solution
is never evidence of competence ([assessment standard](../governance/assessment-standard.md)).

Public archives of earlier years were found by the research pass but not read in depth: Elkies, Math 55a
Fall 2017 (https://people.math.harvard.edu/~elkies/M55a.17/, fetched, notes and problem sets, licence
not stated) and a Siu Math 55a Fall 2003 syllabus (https://abel.math.harvard.edu/archive/55a_fall_03/syllabus/index.html,
Harvard copyright notice). Status: **partially verified (exists; contents not reviewed)**. No open licence
was found; personal study only.

### What the honours lectures cover (verbatim from the Canvas pages, abbreviated)

Math 55a, selected lectures: L1 groups, examples; L2 set theory, subgroups, homomorphisms; L3 subgroups
of Z, cyclic groups, equivalence relations, cosets; L4 cosets, normal subgroups, quotient groups, exact
sequences; L5 the symmetric group, free groups, rings and fields; L6-L8 vector spaces, bases, dimension,
linear maps and matrices, change of basis, rank and dimension formula; L9-L12 duals, linear operators,
eigenvalues, Jordan normal form, characteristic and minimal polynomials; L14-L17 bilinear forms, inner
products, spectral theorem; L18-L20 tensor products, trace, exterior algebra, determinant; L21-L22
finitely generated abelian groups, group actions, Burnside's lemma, conjugacy classes; L23-L26 finite
subgroups of SO(3), conjugacy in S_n and A_n, Sylow theorems; L28-L30 Sylow proofs, presentations;
L31-L37 representations and characters.

Math 55b, selected lectures: L1-L9 metric and topological spaces, continuity, compactness, completeness,
separation axioms; L10-L18 quotient topology, homotopy, fundamental group, covering spaces,
Seifert-van Kampen; L19-L20 continuity of real functions, sequences and series, power series, the Riemann
integral; L21-L23 Arzela-Ascoli, Stone-Weierstrass, Fourier series; L23-L26 differentiation in several
variables, inverse and implicit function theorems, integration in several variables, differential forms;
L27 Stokes; L27-L37 complex analysis.

## B. The Harvard discrete mathematics page (Math 55, Spring 2018)

URL: https://people.math.harvard.edu/~cmwang/teaching/55/ . **This is not the honours sequence.**

- The page itself shows only the title "Math 55", Worksheets 1 to 21 with solutions, and a midterm.
- It was identified as "MATH 55 - Discrete Mathematics, Spring 2018" from the author's teaching page
  (https://people.math.harvard.edu/~cmwang/teaching/), where the author, then a PhD student, lists it.
  The lecturing instructor and the textbook are **not stated** in anything read.
- The topics read were logic and truth tables, counting and the binomial theorem, modular arithmetic and
  the pigeonhole principle, and Euler paths. Only a handful of the PDFs were opened.
- Licence: not stated. Verification: **partially verified**.

Judgement (not sourced): modest relevance to Numbers and Sets, as extra routine practice with worked
solutions on logic, counting and modular arithmetic. Introductory and computational; Cambridge treats
these more rigorously. Graph theory is outside Part IA. Low priority.

## C. Enrichment map to Part IA

Category: **same-deeper** = the same material treated more abstractly or generally; **adjacent** = nearby
but not the same; **beyond** = outside Part IA (cross-checked against the Schedules 2026-27, which place
the topic in the course named). Timing is a recommendation (judgement) for the curriculum director.

### Numbers and Sets (IA-NS-001 to IA-NS-022)

No Math 55 lecture is devoted to this course. 55a L2-L3 (set theory, equivalence relations) touches
IA-NS-003 to IA-NS-007, and Halmos, *Naive Set Theory* (recommended, not required, on the 55a page) is
adjacent to sets, relations, functions and countability. The 2018 discrete mathematics page (section B)
is adjacent for logic, counting and modular arithmetic. There is no honest lecture-level mapping for
number systems, elementary number theory or countability, and none is forced.

### Differential Equations, Probability, Vector Calculus, Dynamics and Relativity

Little or no overlap with the Math 55 topic lists. The only loose link is 55b L23-L27 (several-variable
calculus, differential forms, Stokes) to Vector Calculus, a much more abstract treatment (adjacent at
best). No mapping is forced. Part IA applied coverage is unaffected by enrichment.

## D. Recommended use (for the director to confirm)

1. After Groups: read the opening group theory of Artin (55a L1-L4, L21-L22) as a second treatment.
2. After Vectors and Matrices: read the matching Axler chapters (already owned); see
   [reading-map.md](reading-map.md) for what Axler does and does not cover.
3. After Analysis I: read the Rudin chapters matching 55b L19-L20 as consolidation; treat metric spaces
   and compactness as a stretch into Part IB.
4. Treat everything labelled "beyond" as optional and not for the diagnostic.
