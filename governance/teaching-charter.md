# Teaching charter

Status: **proposed in Baseline v1, awaiting curriculum-director approval.**

## Purpose

A long-term programme of rigorous study based on the Cambridge Mathematical Tripos **Part IA**, for a
student pursuing a foundation in pure and applied mathematics with an eventual interest in advanced
mathematics and computational mathematical physics.

The programme prioritises, in this order:

1. **mathematical maturity** - reading, writing and criticising proofs;
2. **independent proof construction** - producing arguments without being led to them;
3. **durable understanding** - knowledge that survives a gap and transfers to new problems;
4. speed and syllabus completion, only after the above.

## Authority hierarchy

1. **Cambridge Schedules** - authoritative for syllabus coverage.
2. **Cambridge Guide to Courses** - supporting information on courses, prerequisites and sequencing.
3. **Primary textbooks** - rigorous development, proofs and exercises.
4. **Harvard Math 55** (the honours sequence 55a/55b) - enrichment in algebra, analysis, abstraction
   and proof construction. It is distinct from the Harvard discrete mathematics course page the
   student also supplied; see `curriculum/enrichment.md`.
5. **Cambridge examination papers** - assessment and independent problem solving.

Material is always labelled one of: **Cambridge-required** (in the Schedules), **supplementary
prerequisite** (needed to read the required material but not in it), or **optional enrichment**.

## Teaching principles

- **Socratic first.** The tutor asks before it tells. Definitions are met through examples and
  non-examples; proofs are reconstructed by the student wherever possible.
- **Struggle is data.** A stalled argument is recorded as evidence of where understanding stops, not
  smoothed over. The tutor gives the smallest hint that restores progress and records it.
- **No credit for passive contact.** Reading, listening and following a worked example are valuable
  but are not evidence of competence (see `assessment-standard.md`).
- **Write it out.** Proofs are written in full by the student. A proof that was only talked through is
  not a constructed proof.
- **Return to things.** Items are revisited after an interval and in new contexts before durable
  mastery is considered.
- **Balance.** Part IA is applied as well as pure. Differential Equations, Probability, Vector
  Calculus and Dynamics and Relativity receive full coverage, not a token pass.
- **Computation is an instrument.** Computational exploration is encouraged where it deepens
  understanding (for example iterating the logistic map, simulating random walks, orbit integration),
  and it never substitutes for the proof.
- **Honesty about gaps.** Weaknesses are written down in `progress/weaknesses.md` with evidence.

## What the repository will not do

- It will not teach: tutoring happens in the ChatGPT conversation.
- It will not assess without evidence, or record mastery that the assessor has not authorised.
- It will not impose a rigid timetable. The sequence in `curriculum/dependencies.md` is a proposal
  informed by prerequisites, to be replaced by a personal plan after the diagnostic assessment.
